Bull case
Micron just reported results six days ago, and its operating margin of 74.6 percent towers above the five-year average of 20 percent, suggesting the artificial intelligence boom is driving genuine pricing power in memory chips. At 14.1 times trailing earnings with a return on equity of 91.8 percent and virtually no debt, the company is throwing off cash at a scale that justifies at least some of this multiple expansion.
 
Bear case
First, the company faces a classic cyclical risk: operating margins at 3.7 times their historical average suggest earnings may already be at peak levels, which means trailing valuation multiples are artificially depressed and misleading about forward value. Second, price targets assume Micron sustains elevated margins and demand, but memory chip cycles are notoriously volatile, and competitive supply increases or AI demand disappointment could compress margins sharply. Third, the 484 percent gain in twelve months already reflects massive optimism, and any guidance miss or macro slowdown could trigger a severe pullback in a stock with elevated positioning.
