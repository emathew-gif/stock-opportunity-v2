Bull case
NVIDIA is delivering outsized returns on shareholder capital with a 110% return on equity and 83% year-over-year revenue growth, backed by durable 75% gross margins that suggest strong pricing power and competitive moats in AI chips. The balance sheet is fortress-like with only 0.15x debt-to-equity and a current ratio of 4.59, providing ample flexibility to invest in capacity and weather downturns.
 
Bear case
The first risk is execution: earnings have beaten expectations so far, but investor expectations are now sky-high and any miss on guidance could trigger sharp repricing. Second, current valuations at 17.4x sales and a 20.8x price-to-book ratio assume the company can sustain this growth trajectory for years, yet semiconductor cycles historically reverse and data center spending can slow if customer capex budgets tighten. Third, a broader market slowdown or recession could crush enterprise spending on AI infrastructure regardless of NVIDIA's competitive position, and rising rates would pressure multiple expansion that now carries the stock.
